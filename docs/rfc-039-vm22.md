RFC-016's machinery is not rebuilt. The accumulated-deficiency roll, the
greatest present value, the scenario reserves and the CTE are the same for
VM-20, VM-21 and VM-22 — because they *are* the same; the chapters differ
in scope, assumptions and floors, not in what a conditional tail
expectation is. What is new here is the floor, the exclusion machinery, and
one finding about where the arithmetic happens.

## Corrected against the Valuation Manual (August 2026)

This RFC shipped without the text. It was then read — NAIC *Valuation
Manual*, 1 January 2026 edition, chapter VM-22 — and **three of the
original modelling choices were wrong**. All three are fixed, and the
corrections are more interesting than the original design.

| § | what the text says | what the first cut did |
|---|---|---|
| 3.A | the aggregate reserve is the SR **plus** the DR **plus** the formulaic reserve, over *disjoint groups* | a **maximum** over components of one block — VM-20's shape |
| 4.B.1 | "The scenario reserve for any given scenario shall not be less than the cash surrender value in aggregate", *then* CTE 70 | floored the CTE result — the floor **outside** the tail |
| 7.C.1 | ratio is `(b − a) / c`, where `c` is the **present value of benefits** | divided by the baseline reserve |

Two things the first cut got right and can now cite: **CTE 70** (§3.D.2),
and the refusal to default the exclusion threshold — §7.C.1 sets it at "the
lesser of 6.0% and the percentage change that would trigger the company's
materiality standard", so a bare 6% default would also have been wrong.

**The aggregate reserve is a sum over a partition.** §3.A adds one reserve
per *group of contracts*: the SR for groups modelled stochastically, the DR
for groups passing the Single Scenario Test, the formulaic reserve for
groups that passed the exclusion test and elected not to model. Those are
partitions of the book, not competing valuations of one block, so "which
component binds" is a VM-20 question with no VM-22 answer.
`AggregateReserve` sums `ReservingGroup`s and reports the composition.

## The finding: the prescribed ordering is not bracketed by the obvious two

A stochastic reserve is a tail statistic over a **book**; a cash surrender
value belongs to a **contract**. There are three places the floor can go:

- **floor outside** — `max(Σf, CTE(ΣX))`: the natural reading;
- **floor inside** — `CTE(max(Σf, ΣX_s))`: what §4.B.1 prescribes;
- **seriatim** — `Σ max(f_i, CTE(X_i))`: contract-by-contract reserving.

Only one inequality holds in general: **floor outside ≤ prescribed**,
because `max(F, X)` dominates both arguments pointwise and a CTE is
monotone. Reading §4.B.1 the natural way *understates* the reserve — and
the gap opens **exactly when the surrender value sits inside the tail**,
above the CTE of the unfloored scenarios and below the worst of them.
Hand-computed, on reserves `[0, 0, 100, 200]` at CTE(50):

| surrender value | floor outside | prescribed | cost of the misreading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 150 | 150 | 0 |
| 50 | 150 | 150 | 0 |
| 120 | 150 | 160 | 10 |
| 150 | 150 | 175 | **25** |
| 190 | 190 | 195 | 5 |
| 250 | 250 | 250 | 0 |

Zero at both extremes, largest in between — and "in between" is where a
deferred annuity's surrender value normally sits against its reserve.

**The finding proper: seriatim is not the conservative end.** Every
actuary's intuition says reserving contract by contract can only be safer
than aggregating. Against the prescribed ordering it is not. Two contracts,
scenario reserves `[0, 0, 0, 150]` each, surrender value 100 each: each
contract's own CTE(50) is 75 — below its own floor — so seriatim reserves
100 + 100 = **200**. Pooled, the scenario reserves are `[0, 0, 0, 300]`,
floored at the aggregate 200, and CTE(50) of `[200, 200, 200, 300]` is
**250**. The aggregate exceeds the sum of the standalone reserves by 50,
with no diversification anywhere in sight.

The mechanism: seriatim gives each contract its own floor *and* its own
tail, while the prescribed calculation applies the **summed** floor to the
**pooled** scenario reserve — so the pool has a tail no individual contract
had. This RFC's original claim that "seriatim is never smaller" was true of
the misreading and is false of the text.

**The original finding survives underneath it.**
`aggregation_decomposition` still splits `seriatim − floor outside` exactly
into a **floor effect** (a sum of maxima dominates a maximum of sums) and a
**diversification effect** (a CTE is subadditive), both non-negative. And
the floor can still eat the diversification benefit entirely: on a block
whose surrender value binds in every scenario, all three placements
collapse to one number and pooling has bought nothing, however uncorrelated
the block. Two perfectly anti-correlated contracts with binding floors get
exactly `0.0` credit for being pooled.

So "our stochastic reserve fell when we aggregated" is still worth nothing
until somebody has checked whether the floor binds — and there is now a
second question behind it: *where did you put the floor?*

## What this module refuses to invent

VM-22 is a dated text, and its *numbers* — the stochastic exclusion
threshold, the prescribed scenario set, the prescribed assumption margins —
belong to it rather than to this repository. So:

- `VM22Basis` is a **dated parameter set**, in the manner of
  `engine/report/market_risk.py` carrying 2015/35 and 2026/269 side by
  side. A valuation is performed under a text; a module that bakes one in
  silently revalues last year's business under this year's rules the moment
  it is upgraded.
- `VM22_2026` now carries the numbers the text states, and cites them:
  `cte_level = 0.70` (§3.D.2) and `sert_cap = 0.06` (§7.C.1).
- `materiality_standard` stays `None`. §7.C.1's threshold is the *lesser*
  of 6.0% and the company's own materiality standard; only the company
  knows the second, and supplying it can only make the test harder to pass.
- The prescribed economic scenarios (VM-20 Appendix 1.F — sixteen, with
  scenario 9 the baseline) and the prescribed assumption sets are still not
  carried. They are dated regulatory data and belong with F2's
  regulation-as-dated-sets work rather than inlined here.

The original refusal was directionally right and is worth keeping in view:
defaulting the threshold to a plausible 6% would still have been wrong,
because the effective threshold is a minimum against a company-specific
number. What the text states is now carried with a citation; what it leaves
to the company is still refused.

## An excluded component is recorded, never merely absent

§7.B gives three routes out of the stochastic reserve — the ratio test, a
demonstration, and a qualified actuary's certification — and all three are
carried.

A reserve that omits its stochastic component because a test was passed and
one that omits it because nobody computed it are the same *absence* and
completely different *reserves*, and the difference is not recoverable from
a number. So a `ReservingGroup` valued any way other than stochastically will not
be accepted without one, and an `Exclusion` must carry its basis:

- a **ratio test** must carry the ratio it computed — an exclusion nobody
  can recheck is an assertion;
- a **certification** must name the actuary — a certification is a person's
  judgement, and an unsigned one is not one.

A component that is both computed and excluded is refused too: an exclusion
is a statement that the number was not needed, not a note attached to one
that was.

Ties in `binding` resolve to the floor, because a tie broken by dictionary
order would move with the input, and the floor is the conservative reading.

## Known deviations, still open

A second pass over the chapter found two more, both recorded in
`docs/sources/naic-vm22-2026.md` and pinned by tests rather than carried
quietly. §3.F.5.a.ii aggregates the deficiency present values across model
segments *before* taking the greatest, and this module takes the greatest
per contract and sums after — `Σ max` against the text's `max Σ`, which
overstates. §4.B.1.a's guidance note says the greatest present value "can
be negative"; RFC-016 floors it at zero, and that floor is shared with
VM-20 and VM-21, so VM-22 needs its own unfloored path rather than a change
underneath two other chapters.

Both are the same shape as the floor-placement error corrected above:
reduce-then-aggregate where the text aggregates-then-reduces. Three
instances of one mistake is a pattern worth naming — it is what this
framework invites, and it is what to look for first in C-workstream items
that touch aggregation.

§4.B.1.a's PIMR is now an argument to `Contract.from_cashflows` rather than
an omission.

## What is not here

- **No prescribed assumption set and no prescribed scenario generator.**
  The module takes net cashflows and earned rates, as RFC-016's does. The
  economic scenarios a valuation must use are prescribed and dated, and
  generating them is a separate item.
- **No Standard Projection Amount** — and the text supports leaving it out
  for now: §3.C says the additional standard projection amount "is only
  required for disclosure purposes pursuant to VM-31", so it is not a
  reserve floor for year-end 2026. It will need building when that changes,
  together with VM-21's, which is also unimplemented.
- **No aggregation groups.** The reserve is computed over the contracts
  passed to it. Which contracts may be grouped is a question the text
  answers and a caller decides; grouping them wrongly is a modelling error
  this module cannot detect, and pretending otherwise would be worse than
  saying so.
- **No stochastic template.** The library's `FixedAnnuity` is a
  deterministic projection; the end-to-end test supplies the scenario
  dimension around it and says so in the test's own docstring rather than
  implying the template produced it.

The one structural check the module *does* make is that contracts in a
group agree on their scenario count, because adding them otherwise would be
adding across different futures.

## Acceptance

`tests/test_vm22.py` — 34 tests. The stochastic reserve is hand-computed on
a four-scenario block at CTE(50); perfectly correlated contracts get no
benefit and anti-correlated ones get the whole of it; the decomposition
identity holds exactly on the miniature blocks, on a 500-scenario
annuity-shaped block, and on forty random ones, with neither term ever
negative.

The refusals are asserted as hard as the arithmetic: a component neither
computed nor excluded, a component both computed and excluded, a reserve
with nothing in it, a certification with no name, a ratio-test exclusion
with no ratio, a ratio test with no threshold, a zero baseline, an empty
adverse set, an empty group, and contracts that disagree about how many
futures they were run on.

A `FixedAnnuity` run from `engine/library` feeds the reserve without an
adapter, and `Contract.from_cashflows` is asserted to be RFC-016's
`scenario_reserves` rather than a second implementation of the deficiency
roll.
